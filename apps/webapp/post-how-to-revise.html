Five revision habits that actually work

Rereading feels productive, but testing yourself teaches you more. Here’s how to study so that it stays.

Study tips · The Knowzone team

Many students read their notes again and again, then feel lost in the exam hall. Reading makes a topic feel familiar, but an exam asks you to bring it back from memory. These five habits train exactly that.

1. Test yourself first

Close your notes and write down what you remember. Then check what you missed. That small struggle is what helps you remember.

2. Space it out

Short sessions across several days beat one long night. Come back to a topic after a day, then after a few days, then after a week.

3. Mix your topics

When you practise only one type of question, you always know which method to use. In an exam you don’t. Mix different topics so you learn to choose.

4. Explain it out loud

Teach the idea to a friend, or to Kora. If you get stuck, you’ve found exactly what to study next.

5. Practise under real conditions

Sit some papers with a timer and no notes. You’ll learn your pace, and exam day will feel less strange.
